Callable 5 of 23 — orchestrate_fit_woe_encoding  (Task 5: Monotonic Binning and WoE
Encoding, Logistic Branch; cell 25, L8654)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inputs: config, zindi_train_df, zindi_y, zindi_frames, zindi_logistic_features,
taiwan_train_df, taiwan_y, taiwan_frames, taiwan_logistic_features, taiwan_continuous,
taiwan_categorical, output_dir.

Processes: (1) fit_monotonic_bins (with helpers woe_from_counts, _merge_bins,
_is_monotone, _enforce_monotonicity, _enforce_min_frequency, _interval_label,
assert_interval_key_uniqueness, _qcut_edges, _bins_from_edges) — 20 initial quantile
bins per continuous feature on training; per-bin WoE from Equation (2); monotonicity
enforced by merging the adjacent pair with closest WoE whenever the sequence changes
direction, iterated to a monotone sequence; minimum bin frequency 5% of training
observations enforced; (2) compute_woe_scores — under the frozen boundaries, bin and
level counts (g_k, b_k, G, B) are recomputed on training and the final WoE per bin and
per categorical level is formed (Equation 2); categories with a zero count in either
class receive the documented neutral WoE = 0 treatment; (3) apply_woe_mapping — each
validation/test observation is binned by the frozen cut points (continuous) or raw
level (categorical) and replaced by the training-learned WoE; unseen levels map to
0.0 and are logged; (4) persist cut_points and woe_map per dataset.

Outputs: frozen cut_points and woe_map dicts (feature -> {bin/level identifier ->
WoE}) and the WoE-transformed matrices X_woe^{train/val/test} for both datasets with
identical column ordering.

Transformation: raw numeric/categorical values -> monotone-risk WoE scores. The
encoding is a pure logistic-branch artefact: it 'preserve[s] the auditable per-feature
decomposition that regulators require of a scorecard'.

Manuscript role: implements Equation (2),
  WoE_k = ln( (g_k/G) / (b_k/B) ),
'where g_k and b_k are the counts of non-default and default observations in bin k of
a binned predictor, and G, B are the corresponding totals' (Section 3.1), together
with Section 5.1's monotonic-binning and neutral-WoE rules ('Rare WoE values
encountered in held-out folds that do not appear in training are assigned a neutral
WoE = 0, preserving the log-odds-zero contribution and preventing information
leakage'). The binning itself is the monotone-risk scorecard machinery of Siddiqi
(2012)/Thomas (2017) that Section 2.1 identifies as the regulatory default.

Callable 6 of 23 — orchestrate_iv_filter_and_ohe  (Task 6: IV Filtering and One-Hot
Encoding, Boosting Branch; cell 26, L9173)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inputs: config, train_df, y, woe_map, cut_points, woe_matrices, partition_frames,
categorical_features, continuous_features, output_dir, verify_manuscript_targets.

Processes: (1) compute_information_values — per feature, IV = sum_k
((g_k/G)-(b_k/B)) * WoE_k using the frozen WoE values on the training partition; (2)
apply_iv_filter — retain features with IV >= 0.02 and filter the WoE matrices to the
retained columns; (3) assert_zindi_iv_targets (when verify_manuscript_targets) — all
ten Zindi features pass, and the three strongest IVs are education_level ~0.97,
cellphone_access ~0.71 and job_type ~0.63 within absolute tolerance 0.02; (4)
fit_one_hot_encoding — pandas.get_dummies(drop_first=False) over the categorical
levels observed in training, sorted unique levels per feature frozen, continuous
features appended numerically; (5) apply_one_hot_encoding — get_dummies per partition
reindexed against the frozen training column list with zero-fill for absent columns,
asserting X_ohe_val.columns.equals(X_ohe_train.columns) and likewise for test; (6)
persist the IV table, retained feature set and frozen one-hot column list.

Outputs: IV table, retained feature set, frozen OHE level/column list, X_ohe^{train/
val/test} matrices with identical column ordering.

Transformation: raw categorical levels -> {0,1} indicator blocks aligned to a frozen
training vocabulary, so 'the boosting model [can] capture nonlinear interactions among
categorical levels' on an independent representation.

Manuscript role: implements the Section 5.1 Information-Value filter ('applying the IV
>= 0.02 feature-selection filter') and the one-hot branch of the two-representation
design — 'for the residual learner, categorical features are one-hot encoded' — with
the Section 4.1 IV facts (education 0.97, cellphone access 0.71, job type 0.63)
asserted as verification targets. The IV retention is applied to the logistic-branch
feature economy only; the boosting branch keeps the full feature set, matching the
manuscript's requirement that the two branches 'consume different feature
representations'.

Callable 7 of 23 — orchestrate_fit_logistic  (Task 7: Fit Logistic Regression Baseline
with C Selection; cell 27, L9600)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inputs: config, X_woe_train, y_train, X_woe_val, y_val, X_woe_test, y_test, output_dir,
feature_names.

Processes: (1) select_C_cv — StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=42);
for each C in {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10} fit LogisticRegression(solver='lbfgs', penalty='l2',
class_weight='balanced', C=C, max_iter=1000) on each fold's training rows, score fold
validation AUC, average the five, select the argmax C; lbfgs convergence failures are
handled by max_iter and logging; (2) fit_final_logistic — refit on the full training
partition with the selected C and extract the coefficient vector beta_hat and
intercept beta_0_hat for adverse-action explanations; (3) predict_lr_partitions —
predict_proba on all three matrices; (4) persist the CV table and coefficients;
record the validation AUC for the later comparison against ~0.854 on test.

Outputs: selected C, fitted estimator, beta_hat, beta_0_hat, p_lr^{train/val/test},
LR validation AUC, CV table (JSON).

Transformation: WoE matrix -> probability vector via the sigmoid link on the linear
predictor.

Manuscript role: implements Equation (1),
  p_LR(x_i) = sigma(beta^T x_i + beta_0),   sigma(z) = (1 + e^{-z})^{-1},
'with beta in R^p fitted by maximum likelihood under balanced class weighting'
(Section 3.1), the Section 5.2 configuration (lbfgs solver, L2, balanced, C grid
{0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}), and the Section 3.1 claim that 'WoE-encoded features and L2
regularization on beta together preserve the auditable per-feature decomposition that
regulators require of a scorecard'. Balanced class weighting is retained 'exactly as
the manuscript specifies' with its probability-scale consequence deferred to Platt
calibration.

Callable 8 of 23 — orchestrate_compute_residuals  (Task 8: Compute Raw Response
Residuals; cell 28, L9907)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inputs: labels (dict of the three label vectors keyed by partition), p_lr (dict of the
three LR probability vectors).

Processes: (1) compute_training_residuals — elementwise r_i = y_i - p_LR(x_i) on the
training partition; (2) compute_partition_residuals — the identical formula on
validation and test (read-only artefacts, retained for evaluation and for the
alternating-minimization procedure, never used to fit the booster); (3)
validate_residual_distribution — assert r in [-1, 1], report mean/std/min/max, and
halt if the training std falls below 1e-3 (a degenerate LR would make residual
learning meaningless).

Outputs: the three residual vectors r^{train/val/test} and the training residual
summary statistics.

Transformation: probability and label vectors -> signed {-1, +1}-bounded residual
regression target, positionally aligned to the partitions.

Manuscript role: implements Equation (3),
  r_i = y_i - p_LR(x_i),
the raw response residual that Section 3.2 adopts deliberately: 'Pearson or deviance
residuals are more standard for GLM diagnostics, but the raw response residual is
adopted here because it provides a direct regression target for the secondary learner
and aligns with the residual-learning paradigm popularized for deep networks by He et
al.' The orchestrator encodes the algebraic implication that residuals are
heteroscedastic by construction and are therefore fitted by squared-error regression,
and the validation rule guards the residual-branch premise itself.

Callable 9 of 23 — orchestrate_fit_residual_xgboost  (Task 9: Fit Residual XGBoost
Learner; cell 29, L10313)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inputs: config, X_ohe_matrices, residuals, p_lr, labels, lr_only_val_auc, output_dir.

Processes: (1) fit_residual_booster with the _HybridAucEarlyStopping monitor —
XGBRegressor(objective='reg:squarederror', n_estimators=400, max_depth=3,
learning_rate=0.03, subsample=0.8, colsample_bytree=0.8, reg_lambda=2), early-stopped
with patience 50 on the validation AUC of the provisional hybrid p_LR + r_hat (per
config; fixed 400 otherwise), seeded; (2) predict_residuals — predict on all three
one-hot matrices at the frozen best iteration, recording the actual tree count; no
NaN assertion; (3) validate_residual_contribution — Pearson correlation between r and
r_hat on training asserted positive (scipy.stats.pearsonr) and the provisional hybrid
validation AUC recorded; (4) persist hyperparameters and scalars.

Outputs: fitted booster, best_iteration (frozen), r_hat^{train/val/test}, correlation,
provisional hybrid validation AUC, persisted JSON.

Transformation: one-hot features -> residual-score vector r_hat(x) = f_GBM(x), the
nonlinear correction to the linear baseline.

Manuscript role: implements Equation (4),
  r_hat(x) = f_GBM(x) = sum_{m=1}^M nu h_m(x),
'with h_m the regression tree fitted at iteration m and nu the learning rate' and the
Section 5.2 configuration (XGBoost reg:squarederror, 300-500 trees, depth 3-4, rate
0.03-0.05, subsample 0.8, colsample_bytree 0.8, lambda 2). The shallow-tree/ low-rate
design is deliberate: 'deeper trees allow the boosting component to dominate the
prediction and erode the interpretability ratio' (Section 5.2), which is why the
orchestrator's early-stopping target is the hybrid AUC and not the residual MSE
alone.

Callable 11 of 23 — orchestrate_select_adaptive_weights  (Task 11: Adaptive Weighting
Hyperparameter Selection; cell 31, L11118)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inputs: config, X_woe_train, y_train, X_ohe_train, selected_C, p_lr, output_dir.

Processes: (1) grid_search_adaptive_weights over the 27 triples (alpha_min in
{0.0, 0.1, 0.2}, alpha_max in {0.3, 0.5, 0.8}, gamma in {0.5, 1.0, 2.0}) with 5-fold
stratified CV: in each fold fit LR, compute fold-training residuals, fit the residual
booster, compute fold-validation u_LR and alpha*, form the hybrid prediction of
Equation (5) and score validation AUC; the manuscript's 3-fold/5-fold inconsistency
is resolved to 5-fold and recorded; (2) freeze_adaptive_weights — recompute u_LR and
alpha* for every observation of all three partitions from the final full-training LR
probabilities (never the fold models) and assert alpha* is non-negative and bounded;
(3) persist the grid table and the selected triple.

Outputs: selected (alpha_min, alpha_max, gamma), per-observation u_LR and alpha*
arrays for train/val/test, grid CV table (JSON).

Transformation: LR probabilities -> per-observation local-uncertainty weights that
scale the residual correction before it enters the hybrid sum.

Manuscript role: implements Equations (8) and (9),
  u_LR(x) = 4 * p_LR(x) * (1 - p_LR(x)) in [0,1],
  alpha*(x) = alpha_min + (alpha_max - alpha_min) * u_LR(x)^gamma, gamma > 0,
of Section 3.4 — 'the weight alpha*(x) in Eq. (5) is allowed to vary with the local
uncertainty of the logistic baseline' — with the Section 5.2 grids and the note that
'static and adaptive weighting hyperparameters are selected via 5-fold and 3-fold
stratified cross-validation respectively' (the orchestrated implementation standardises
on 5-fold and logs the deviation).

Callable 12 of 23 — orchestrate_build_hybrid_and_rho  (Task 12: Hybrid Prediction and
Interpretability Decomposition; cell 32, L11499)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inputs: config, p_lr, r_hat, alpha_star, labels, output_dir, verify_manuscript_targets.

Processes: (1) compute_hybrid_probabilities — p_Hybrid(x) = clip(p_LR(x) + alpha*(x)
* r_hat(x), 0, 1) via numpy.clip with NaN and range assertions, storing the unclipped
additive components for the rho computation; (2) compute_rho_and_regions — the ratio
of Equation (6) from the unclipped components, with the zero-denominator guard
(rho = 1 when the denominator falls below 1e-12), and region assignment by the
thresholds of Equation (7) on the UNcalibrated hybrid probabilities exactly as the
manuscript specifies; (3) aggregate_region_stats on the test partition — region
shares, within-region positive rates, mean rho per region and the full rho
distribution, verified against Table 3 (6.4% / 34.1% / 59.4%; 53.8% / 19.7% / 6.5%;
+-1 percentage point) and the bimodality assertion when enabled; (4) persist region
statistics.

Outputs: p_hybrid^{train/val/test} (uncalibrated), rho vectors, region assignment
vectors, test region-statistics table (JSON).

Transformation: probability, residual and weight vectors -> a single hybrid score plus
a per-prediction auditability scalar and a three-level region label.

Manuscript role: implements Equations (5), (6) and (7),
  p_Hybrid(x) = clip(p_LR(x) + alpha*(x) r_hat(x), 0, 1),
  rho(x) = |p_LR(x)| / ( |p_LR(x)| + |alpha*(x) r_hat(x)| ) in [0,1],
  Region(x) = Fully interpretable if rho >= 0.9; Partially interpretable if
  0.7 <= rho < 0.9; ML-driven if rho < 0.7,
the paper's second methodological pillar. The orchestrator also encodes Section 3.3's
flagged property: because rho is defined 'on the probability scale rather than the
logit scale', 'borrowers for whom p_LR(x) is large (close to one) tend to fall in the
fully-interpretable region', so the empirical concentration of high-rate borrowers in
that region is a joint algebraic-calibration property, not an independent discovery
— the region statistics are therefore interpreted with that coupling explicitly in
view.

Callable 13 of 23 — orchestrate_fit_platt  (Task 13: Platt Calibration; cell 33,
